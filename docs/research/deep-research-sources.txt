Former OpenAI, Tesla engineer Andrej Karpathy starts AI education platform

Andrej Karpathy, a former engineer at OpenAI and Tesla, has launched an AI-based education platform called Eureka Labs. Drawing on his extensive experience in artificial intelligence, Karpathy announced that the platform will feature AI teaching assistants designed to aid educators in course creation and guide students through the learning process. The first offering from Eureka Labs is "LLM101n," an undergraduate-level course in which students learn to train their own scaled-down AI models. Karpathy—who was a founding member of OpenAI in 2015 and later led Tesla’s Autopilot division—described Eureka Labs as the result of his two-decade-long passion for both AI and education. He holds a PhD from Stanford University and has previously shared educational content online related to AI and problem-solving techniques such as Rubik’s cube tutorials. The launch of this platform aligns with the broader trend of integrating generative AI into digital learning since the advent of tools like ChatGPT.

Andrej Karpathy

Andrej Karpathy is a pivotal figure in modern artificial intelligence, known for both his research contributions and his prowess as an educator. A founding member of OpenAI in 2015, Karpathy played a key role as a research scientist. He later led Tesla’s computer vision efforts beginning in 2017 and rejoined OpenAI in 2023 to contribute to the development of GPT-4. However, his broader influence may stem from his exceptional ability to teach neural networks and deep learning principles. His lecture videos have garnered over 800,000 views, and his instructional YouTube content has reached millions. Recognized for simplifying complex AI topics, Karpathy credits his physics background for his clarity and teaching style, inspired by renowned physicist Richard Feynman. In July 2024, he launched Eureka Labs, a venture aimed at creating an AI-native educational platform. Its inaugural offering will be an undergraduate-level AI course co-developed and assisted by AI, reflecting Karpathy's commitment to innovating education at the intersection of technology and human learning.

Everything you need to know about the GitHub Copilot pricing changes

GitHub is transitioning GitHub Copilot to a new usage-based billing system starting June 1, 2026, replacing the current Premium Request Units (PRU) model with GitHub AI Credits. This change comes as GitHub addresses rising costs due to enhanced Copilot features that have increased computational demand. Under the new system, users will consume credits based on token usage—including inputs, outputs, and cached tokens—per model API rates. Features like code completions and "Next Edit" suggestions won't consume credits, but code review will also use GitHub Actions minutes. Pricing for Copilot subscriptions remains unchanged: Copilot Business at $19/month and Copilot Enterprise at $39/month, each including equivalent value in AI credits. As a transition aid, Business and Enterprise users will receive bonus monthly credits for June through August. Pooled credit usage across organizations will allow efficient allocation, especially for managing heavy and light users. Admins will have tools to set spending caps and monitor usage. For individuals, Copilot Pro and Pro+ users will receive equivalent AI credits to their $10 or $39/month plans. Annual subscribers remain on legacy pricing until expiration, after which they'll convert to free or monthly plans. GitHub has already begun preparations for the shift, including temporary usage limits.
